# Future Games Balance Notes

This file records mechanic findings before any staged candidate receives public UI work. The goal is to kill dominant strategies and cosmetic mechanics while changes are still cheap.

## Traffic Control

### Found
The first deterministic model used roughly symmetric independent arrivals. A fixed switch cadence around 18–20 ticks could outperform a simple queue-aware policy.

### Fixed
Traffic now arrives in deterministic asymmetric demand waves. A queue-aware policy must beat the best tested fixed cadence by at least 20% and survive at least 3× longer than never switching.

## Switchyard Daily

### Found
Targets were initially generated by selecting a random action and observing the resulting depot. Because two actions can lead to the same depot when one branch is inactive, the current route could be overrepresented.

### Fixed
Targets are now sampled uniformly from **distinct reachable depots**. A visible-state oracle solves every tested seed, while blind single-button policies almost always strike out before ten trains.

## Market Maker

### Found
Lean-long/lean-short changed displayed quotes, but fill probabilities were mostly independent of those displayed prices. Inventory skew was therefore partly cosmetic.

### Fixed
Fill probability now depends on actual bid/ask distance from fair value. Inventory-aware skew materially changes order flow and reduces ending inventory risk. The risk penalty was increased so high-flow inventory accumulation cannot dominate on gross spread capture alone.

## Supply Chain Shock

### Found
The first scenario pack treated most choices as independent point adjustments. A player could choose “Activate alternate capacity” during a supplier shutdown even after never qualifying alternate supply. Safety stock also had little later mechanical value.

### Fixed
The scenario engine now supports state requirements and dynamic impacts. Alternate capacity requires sufficient resilience from earlier preparation. Safety stock is actually consumed during the port delay and cushions service/backlog damage. Exhaustive legal-path scoring keeps dual sourcing and safety stock competitive rather than making one preparation choice universal.

## Chip Fab

### Check
A capacity/utilization-first game would teach the wrong lesson if “run flat-out” or raw starts dominated the score.

### Gate
All 81 decision paths are exhaustively scored. Running the lithography bottleneck flat-out must remain materially worse on average than adding capacity or improving scheduling, and the total score spread must remain meaningful.

## Power Grid Dispatcher

### Found
With reliability only included as one weighted final metric, temporarily running dangerously tight reserves could be rewarded because later choices recovered the final reliability number. Storage also behaved too much like a generic point pool and could be overused.

### Fixed
Reliability/reserve violations now create immediate operating penalties that survive later recovery. Storage responses have minimum-energy requirements, and the initial energy budget is low enough that the player cannot choose all three storage-heavy responses in one day.

## Shared lesson

A visible number changing is not enough to make a game decision meaningful. The staged audits now explicitly test for:

- causal linkage between the player's visible choice and system behavior;
- path dependence when preparation should matter;
- finite resources staying finite;
- skill signals beating fixed or blind policies;
- hard constraints being treated as constraints rather than optional score flavor.
